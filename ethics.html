Code of Ethics

The standards of professional conduct expected of all WIHT-credentialed practitioners, in every country and context of practice.

Version 1.0 · Effective January 2025 · Issued by Saber Consciente Worldwide LLC, Wyoming, USA

I. Preamble

The World Institute of Holistic Therapists (WIHT) is a private credentialing body dedicated to advancing the practice of holistic and integrative therapy worldwide. This Code of Ethics establishes the minimum standards of conduct required of all practitioners who hold a WIHT credential or are listed in the WIHT Global Directory.

All WIHT members agree to uphold these principles as a condition of their membership and certification.

II. Scope of Practice

WIHT-credentialed practitioners are authorized to offer holistic, complementary, and integrative therapy services within the scope of their specific training.

  • Accurate representation: Practitioners must accurately represent their qualifications, credentials, and scope of practice to all clients at all times.
  • No clinical diagnosis: Practitioners shall not issue clinical diagnoses, formulate diagnostic hypotheses, or use diagnostic screening instruments for clinical purposes unless they hold the separate regulatory qualifications required by their jurisdiction.
  • Regulated titles: Practitioners shall not use titles reserved by law for licensed professions — including "Psychologist," "Psychiatrist," "Licensed Counselor," or equivalents — without holding the applicable license in their jurisdiction.
  • Referral obligation: Practitioners must refer clients to licensed healthcare professionals whenever a client's condition requires clinical assessment or medical intervention.

III. Respect and Non-Discrimination

  • Practitioners shall treat all clients with dignity and respect, without discrimination on the basis of race, ethnicity, gender, sexual orientation, religion, nationality, age, or disability.
  • Practitioners shall not exploit the trust, dependency, or vulnerability of any client.
  • Practitioners shall maintain appropriate professional boundaries at all times.

IV. Confidentiality

  • Client privacy: Practitioners shall maintain the confidentiality of all client information, disclosing information only with informed written consent or as required by applicable law.
  • Data protection: Practitioners who operate digitally must comply with applicable data protection laws in their jurisdiction, including LGPD (Brazil), GDPR (EU/UK), and CCPA (California, USA) where applicable.

V. Professional Conduct

  • Practitioners shall not misrepresent the WIHT credential as a government-issued license or regulatory certification.
  • Practitioners shall not engage in false, misleading, or deceptive advertising.
  • Practitioners shall pursue ongoing professional development and keep their knowledge current.
  • Practitioners shall cooperate with any WIHT inquiry into alleged ethical violations.

VI. Compliance with Local Law

Each WIHT practitioner is solely and fully responsible for complying with the professional regulations, licensing requirements, and laws applicable in their country, state, or region of practice. WIHT credentials do not override or supersede local regulatory requirements.

In Brazil: the practice of clinical psychology and the issuance of psychological diagnoses are regulated by the Conselho Federal de Psicologia (CFP). In the United States: professional titles such as Licensed Psychologist, LPC, or LCSW are regulated at the state level. Practitioners are responsible for understanding and following the rules of their specific jurisdiction.

VII. Violations and Sanctions

Violations of this Code may result in suspension or permanent revocation of WIHT membership and credentials. WIHT reserves the right to remove any practitioner from the Global Directory upon finding evidence of ethical misconduct.
